15:00–16:00Toru KajigayaTokyo University of Science
Equivariant realizations of Hermitian symmetric spaces of noncompact type
AbstractLet \(M = G/K\) be a Hermitian symmetric space of noncompact type. We provide a way of constructing \(K\)-equivariant embeddings from \(M\) to its tangent space at the origin by using the polarity of the \(K\)-action. As an application, we reconstruct the \(K\)-equivariant holomorphic embedding called the Harish-Chandra realization and the \(K\)-equivariant symplectomorphism constructed by Di Scala–Loi and Roos, under appropriate identifications of spaces. Moreover, we characterize the holomorphic/symplectic embedding of \(M\) by means of the polarity of the \(K\)-action. Furthermore, we show that a special class of totally geodesic submanifolds in \(M\) is realized as either linear subspaces or bounded domains of linear subspaces in the tangent space by the \(K\)-equivariant embeddings. We also construct a \(K\)-equivariant holomorphic symplectic embedding of an open dense subset of the compact dual into its tangent space at the origin, as a dual of the holomorphic/symplectic embedding of \(M\). This talk is based on joint work with Takahiro Hashinaga.
16:15–17:15Shunsuke SaitoTokyo University of Science
On polytopal sufficient conditions for uniform relative K-polystability and relative K-instability of polarized toric varieties
AbstractIt is well known that there is a one-to-one correspondence between polarized toric varieties and integral convex polytopes. Under this correspondence, uniform relative K-polystability/relative K-instability of varieties can be completely described by polytopes and convex functions. In this talk, I will introduce a condition on polytopes that leads to uniform relative K-polystability. I will also explain a result related to the polytopal sufficient condition for relative K-instability of toric Fano varieties proved by Yotsutani–Zhou. The contents of the talk are based on joint work with Yasufumi Nitta (Tokyo University of Science).

15:00–16:00Shota HamanakaChuo University
Closed Ricci flow with integral or pointwise bound of the scalar curvature
AbstractIn this talk, we will discuss extension problems for the Ricci flow with some sort of bounded geometric quantity. In 1982, Hamilton introduced the Ricci flow, which deforms Riemannian metrics in the direction of the Ricci tensor; one hopes that the Ricci flow deforms any Riemannian metric to some canonical metric. He also proved that the Ricci flow equation on a closed manifold has a unique short-time solution for any initial metric. The next immediate question is the so-called maximal existence time of the Ricci flow with respect to the initial metric. Hamilton proved that \(T < +\infty\) is the maximal existence time of a closed Ricci flow \((M, g(t))\) (\(t \in [0,T)\), \(\dim M \geqq 2\)) if and only if the norm of the Riemannian curvature tensor is unbounded as \(t \to T\). Therefore a uniform bound for the norm of the Riemannian curvature tensor on \(M \times [0,T)\) is enough to extend the Ricci flow smoothly over \(T\). Other known sufficient conditions for extending the flow smoothly are, for example, a uniform bound of the norm of the Ricci curvature tensor on \(M \times [0,T)\) (by Sesum) or certain integral bounds of geometric quantities rather than pointwise ones (by Wang). Di Matteo generalized Wang's results using mixed integral norms (in space and time) parametrized by \((\alpha, \beta) \in (1, \infty)\). In the talk, we will give a new extension result for the closed four-dimensional Ricci flow under the conditions corresponding to \((\alpha, \beta) = (p, +\infty)\) (\(p > 2\)) and \((+\infty, 1)\). If there is time, we will also discuss the condition \(\sup_{M \times [0,T)} |R| < +\infty\) (a uniform bound of the scalar curvature).
16:15–17:15Yohei SakuraiSaitama University
Liouville theorems for harmonic map heat flow along ancient super Ricci flow via reduced geometry
AbstractThis talk is based on joint work with Keita Kunikawa (Utsunomiya University). We have obtained a Liouville theorem for the heat equation along ancient super Ricci flow via Perelman's reduced geometry. Recently, we generalized the target spaces and formulated several Liouville theorems for harmonic map heat flow. In this talk, I will introduce such Liouville theorems. In our Liouville theorems, we impose a growth condition concerning Perelman's reduced distance. For non-positively curved target spaces, our growth condition is sharp. On the other hand, for positively curved target spaces, it is not clear whether our growth condition is sharp. However, in the static case the growth condition can be improved, and it is almost sharp in view of the example constructed by Schoen–Uhlenbeck. I will discuss the sharpness in more detail in the talk.

15:00–16:00Tomoki FujiiTokyo University of Science
Graphical translating solitons for the mean curvature flow and isoparametric functions
AbstractIn this talk, I will discuss the condition for the graph of a function over a Riemannian manifold \(M\), whose level sets give an isoparametric foliation, to be a translating soliton for the mean curvature flow. Such a translating soliton is given as the graph of a function that is the composition of an isoparametric function over \(M\) and a solution of a certain ordinary differential equation. In addition, I will explain the classification of the shapes of such translating solitons in the case where the Riemannian manifold \(M\) is the \(n\)-dimensional sphere (\(n \geqq 2\)).
16:15–17:15Takuma TomihisaWaseda University
The Rarita–Schwinger operator and some related topics
AbstractThe Rarita–Schwinger fields play an important role in physics by describing the gravitino, and the Rarita–Schwinger operator is important for defining these fields. Recently, in mathematics, there has been much research on the Rarita–Schwinger operator, the “spin-3/2 version” of the Dirac operator. In this talk, we present some results on (1) the spectra of the Rarita–Schwinger operators on some symmetric spaces (joint work with Yasushi Homma), and (2) the Rarita–Schwinger fields on nearly Kähler manifolds (joint work with Soma Ohno).
